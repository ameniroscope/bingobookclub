The Vampyre & Carmilla
Two Gothic Novellas · Polidori & Le Fanu

The Vampyre

Born from a single stormy night at the Villa Diodati in 1816, when Lord Byron challenged his companions — among them Mary Shelley, soon to write Frankenstein — to each write a ghost story. Polidori, Byron's personal physician, took a fragment of Byron's own idea and turned it into The Vampyre: the story that gave literature its first aristocratic, seductive vampire, Lord Ruthven, modeled pointedly on Byron himself. Published in 1819, it predates Dracula by nearly eighty years and effectively invented the vampire as we still picture him today.

Carmilla

Serialized in 1871–72, a full quarter-century before Dracula — and a clear influence on it. Le Fanu's story follows the lonely Laura and the strange, magnetic girl who comes to stay in her family's crumbling Styrian schloss. Told with a slow, dreamlike dread, it's remembered too for the frank, romantic charge between Laura and Carmilla, making it one of the earliest openly queer narratives in gothic fiction.
