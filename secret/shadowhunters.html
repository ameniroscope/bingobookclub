I hope you are ready because this is extensive research and information on the amazingness — nay, the masterpiece — that is The Shadowhunter Chronicles by Cassandra Clare. The procrastination stroke hard, the exhaustion hit harder, and I went into fangirling chatterbox mode, my guy. So make yourself a coffee, sit back, and let me explain this series of books that unfortunately took over my life years ago and that I cannot, for the life of me, forget.

First of all: please remember that these books gave us this scene, which deserves so much more than the movie it's stuck in.

To be fair, the movie is a decent introduction to the vibe of the world. And the vibe is this: most magical creatures exist — vampires, werewolves, faeries, warlocks — but they're hidden from mere mortals under a "glamour." To protect those mortals, an angel created little angel-human warriors to fight demons. They're called Shadowhunters, and they're basically humans with fight and badassery in their blood. Plus they have runes: special tattoos that give them power.

☠ the source material ☠

Now that you know the vibes, here's the talk about the beast, the legend, the books.

🗽 The Mortal Instruments (TMI)
2007 · New York · 6 books
It all started here. Six books about a "not like other girls" girl who is, in fact, exactly like every other dystopian-era main character — Clary. She learns that not only are demons real, but she comes from a long line of Shadowhunters and thus has immense power and badassery.
✧ the good
  • immensely bingeable
  • great nerdy side characters
  • sets up the entire world
✧ the bad
  • suffers from "pick me girl" energy
  • incest plotline. #mygodwhy
⚙️ The Infernal Devices (TID)
Victorian era · London · 3 books
Follows Tessa, who discovers she has shapeshifting powers, gets kidnapped by evil sisters, and is saved by two (of course, gorgeous) Shadowhunters. Everyone seems to prefer this trilogy — it is not my favorite, but I get it.
✧ the good
  • the only love triangle ever done right
  • steampunk-est Victorian London, automatons and all
✧ the bad
  • did not love the main character
  • conflict resolution too easy — #deusexmachina
🌫️ The Last Hours (TLA)
Edwardian era · London · 3 books
Follows the kids of the Infernal Devices generation. You read this one only for the characters, the angst, and the queerness of it all. One of the main guys, Matthew — oh my god. Cassandra Clare is genuinely good at writing characters and relationships with all the messiness of dumb teenagers who have to save the world.
✧ the good
  • the character dynamics. all of them.
  • Matthew Fairchild, obviously
✧ the bad
  • plot is mostly "oh no, a demon — let's kill it. oh no, another demon…"
  • only fun if you're here for the vibes, not the stakes
🌴 The Dark Artifices (TDA)
2012 · Los Angeles · 3 books
Takes place a few years after TMI. Follows Emma, an orphan who is just so mad at the world and has sworn to find her parents' killer, and Julian, the best friend who had to take on the caregiver role for his siblings way too early. Spoiler: yes, this is clearly a best-friends-to-lovers story, but I love that trope, what can I say.
✧ the good
  • the friendship. the sibling dynamics.
  • murder mystery element
  • the faeries are genuinely cool
✧ the bad
  • a weird parallel-universe plotline I never fully got

↳ this is my favorite storyline. what can I say.
